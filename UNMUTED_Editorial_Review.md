# UNMUTED (把英语说出来) — Independent Editorial Review of the English

**Scope and method.** I read the whole main manuscript: front matter, Ch01–20, Appendices B, C-1/2/3, G, H and I. I used the companion document only to cross-check claims, not to review its research content.

**Limits you should know about:**
- Word counts below are my manual estimates, not machine counts. Please run `wc` or a script before acting on any figure.
- I did not verify any reference, DOI or CEFR page number. The companion itself says 17 of 29 are still unchecked.
- I did not see the illustrations. Comic captions are reviewed as text only.
- "Paragraph numbers" in locators are my own counts of the text as supplied.

**Variety of English.** The book leans British (labelled, levelled, apologise, queue, maths, takeaway) but also uses American forms (mailbox, pantry, chili). I treated both as acceptable and flag only inconsistency, not correctness.

**Severity scale (from your brief).** Critical = incorrect English or misleading instruction. Major = clearly unnatural, serious contextual problem, or significant inconsistency. Minor = small improvement. Optional = stylistic alternative.

---

## A. Executive Summary

The English in the stories and dialogue is the book's strongest asset. It is natural, controlled, funny, and almost free of error. Apart from the deliberately wrong "learner" lines, I found no ungrammatical sentence in the narrative passages and no Chinglish in the narrator's or characters' English. Dialogue such as Jake's "that's the calmest angry email I've ever not received" and Zoe's "You didn't tell a joke. You filed a report." reads like real speech, not like a textbook.

The problems are concentrated in four places, and none of them is sentence-level grammar:

1. **A few explanatory glosses are wrong or misleading.** Examples: *across from*, *hits different*, the *This is / I'm* rule, and an invented Chinese idiom in Ch03.
2. **Some demonstrations do not demonstrate what the lesson claims.** The worst cases are Ch18 ("same message, three outfits" shows three different messages), Ch17, Ch08 and Ch06.
3. **Continuity and arithmetic slips.** These include the label mystery told three ways, "a year ago" for events a few weeks old, a name conflict, and several wrong word counts.
4. **The back matter's numbers do not match the pages they summarise.** The model essays are far longer than their stated length. Appendix G's "Active" column is padded with items the chapters themselves classify as recognition-only or as counter-examples. Appendix H breaks its own lemma rule.

**Tally.** 2 Critical, 18 Major, about 25 Minor/Optional items. Almost every Critical or Major item is a targeted fix. Nothing requires rewriting the book's voice.

## B. Critical and Major Issues

### Critical

**B-1 · Critical · Ch02 §2.2 ("空间介词的 3D 镜头法") and §2.5 · essential**
- **Original:** "动态三剑客：*right above* 正上方、*across from* 穿过到对面、*past* 路过继续走。"
- **Problem:** *across from* does not mean "crossing to the other side". It means *opposite* (static). The movement word is *across* (*across the courtyard*). *Right above* is also static, so calling the group "动态" is wrong.
- **Why it matters:** Ch02 §2.4 uses *across from* correctly ("right across from my door"), and Ch03 §3.5 C glosses *across* correctly. So the gloss contradicts the book's own examples and will make learners say "across from the courtyard" for a crossing.
- **Fix:** Rename the group "位置与路线三剑客" and split it:
  - *right above / across from* = where something is (static).
  - *past / across* = how you move (path).
  - Keep *across from = opposite* and *across = to the other side* on two separate lines.

**B-2 · Critical · Ch06 §6.3, §6.5 B, G-06 #10 · essential**
- **Original:** "近亲 *hits different* 更年轻，意思是「这次感觉和以前不一样」——常带点说不清的怀旧。" Later: "`hits different`（年轻口语，别用于夸人）".
- **Problem:** *hits different* does not mean "feels different from last time". It means *feels uniquely good or intense* (*this song hits different at night*). It is not tied to nostalgia. It is also regularly used about people, so "别用于夸人" is not accurate.
- **Fix:** "*hits different*：（口语）比平常更到位 / 更有感觉，如 *Rain hits different when you're home.* 语气随意，书面语少用。" Delete the claim about people and nostalgia. Also move it out of the G-06 "Active" list (see B-16).

### Major

**B-3 · Major · Ch01 §1.2 · essential**
- **Original:** "`This is` 既能指物，也能当面或电话里介绍人（*This is Jake.*）；自报姓名才用 `I'm`。"
- **Problem:** The sentence is ambiguous, and "自报姓名才用 `I'm`" is too strict. On the phone, native speakers identify themselves with *This is Jake (speaking)*. A learner could conclude that *Hi, this is Zhou* on a call is wrong.
- **Fix:** "介绍**别人**：*This is Jake.*（当面或电话）。当面自我介绍：*I'm Jake.* 打电话报自己的名字：*This is Jake.* 也常见。"

**B-4 · Major · Ch03 §3.3 · essential**
- **Original:** "英语里 *I'm all ears*（我洗耳恭听）和「把耳朵换成眼睛」的中文说法撞得很妙——都在说「我把注意力全给你了」。"
- **Problem:** "把耳朵换成眼睛" is not a Chinese idiom I can identify. The Chinese counterpart is 洗耳恭听. This is an unsupported cultural claim, and it appears in a "课外科普" box.
- **Fix:** Either delete the sentence or write "和中文的「洗耳恭听」几乎是一对。"

**B-5 · Major · Ch02 §2.2 / §2.6 Q④ / answer key / comic table · essential**
- **Problem:** "三档礼貌度滑杆" lists four items:
  - 日常点单
  - 怕添麻烦（我做）
  - 怕添麻烦（请你做）
  - 协商维权
- Q④ asks why *Please give me…* is not "第 2 档（协商档）". The answer key says the three tiers are *Could I get… / Would you mind if I… / There seems to be a mix-up*, which makes the 协商 tier the third. Comic row 3 says "协商档 + 第 2 档".
- **Fix:** Decide on three tiers (merge the two "怕添麻烦" lines), number them 1–3 once, and make Q④, the key and the comic table use the same numbers.

**B-6 · Major · Ch03 §3.1 vs §3.4 vs answer key · essential**
- **Original (3.1):** "原来 activity room 在**第三个街区**那栋楼的三层，不在咖啡店楼上。"
- **Original (3.4):** "Go past the bike shed, across the courtyard, and it's on your left, above the little coffee place. Not here."
- **Original (key ①):** "活动室在第三个街区那栋楼、小咖啡店上方，不在本楼。"
- **Problem:** Three issues in one scene:
  - 3.1 says "not above the coffee shop"; 3.4 and the key say it is above a coffee place.
  - Ch02 already establishes that 22B's pantry is above the coffee shop, so "above the little coffee place" is ambiguous in this setting.
  - "Third block" (a street block) is contradicted by directions that stay inside one courtyard ("past the shed, across the courtyard").
- **Why it matters:** The chapter teaches *A or B?* clarification, and the scene's own geography is internally inconsistent.
- **Fix:** Pick one geography. Suggested: the activity room is "on the third floor of the building across the courtyard". The two options Zhou offers become *the third floor of this building or the third floor of the other one*. Rename the "third block" distinction if you keep it, and align 3.1, 3.4, the key, the comic captions and the skeleton.

**B-7 · Major · Ch06 §6.1 / §6.4 · essential**
- **Original:** Jake: "Why is the small one more expensive?" Zhou: "It's a bit pricier, but it's worth it — the small one has half the noodles. So per bowl, the big one is cheaper."
- **Problem:** The logic does not hold.
  - Jake says the small cup costs more, so the big one is cheaper in absolute terms.
  - Zhou then says "it's a bit pricier" (which cup?) and argues the big one is cheaper per bowl.
  - If the big cup were absolutely cheaper and bigger, "a bit pricier, but worth it" would be meaningless.
- **Fix:** Make the situation coherent. Example: the big cup costs a little more in total. Jake asks "Why is the big one so much bigger than the price difference?" Or keep Jake's question and change the line to "It's not much more, and it's twice the noodles." Then the whiteboard line *It's a bit pricier, but it's worth it* refers unambiguously to the big cup. Update the comic, G-06 and I-06 to match.

**B-8 · Major · Ch05 §5.1/5.4, Ch08 §8.1–8.4 · essential**
- **Problem:** The running gag does not parse as an apology.
  - The note says "Sorry — those were my noodles". But the noodles *were* Zhou's, so there is nothing to apologise for.
  - Jake's reply "I know. I drank the coffee." is a non-sequitur.
  - The model "认/因/补" apology "That was my mistake. I set the cup down and went for coffee… I'll label my own cup from now on, and keep my hands off other people's lids" apologises for something Zhou did not do. He never touched anyone's lid.
- **Why it matters:** Ch08's lesson (own it / one reason / repair) is taught with an example where there is no offence, which weakens the teaching.
- **Fix:** Give Zhou a real small fault, for example he took Jake's coffee cup by mistake, or he forgot the Thursday task. Reword the note as "Sorry — I made a fuss. Those were my noodles after all." Also fix the arithmetic: 8.1 says "不到二十个词" for a sentence that is about 25 words, and 8.4 has Zoe say "Twenty words" for a speech of about 34 (see C-10).

**B-9 · Major · Ch01, Ch05, Ch08 (label continuity and answer keys) · essential**
- **Problem:** The label mystery is told three times with different logic.
  - 1.1: Jake says "That's mine, I think." The note is his, and the lid carries it.
  - 1.4: Jake "arrives" ("The door opened"), yet explains he had labelled his cup earlier. His explanation is "I labelled my coffee cup, then peeled the note off and left it on the table" — if he peeled it off, the cup is unlabelled.
  - 5.4: Jake reveals the same explanation again as if for the first time ("Turns out I labelled my coffee…").
  - 8.4: a third version ("It was labelled Jake, so I assumed the cup was mine…").
- **Answer keys:** Ch01 ① and Ch05 ② say the note was "贴在 Jake 的咖啡杯上". The stories say it was stuck to the noodle **lid** after being left on the table.
- **Also unexplained:** why "Jake" is written on the whiteboard (Ch01), and the timeline (Ch01 at 11:47 vs Ch05 "11:30 / last Friday" retelling the same event, then "第二天" in Ch02).
- **Fix:** Decide once what happened and who learns it when. Suggested: Jake labelled his cup, left the note on the table, then went to fetch coffee. Ch01 resolves it in one line. Ch05 becomes Zhou *retelling* the Ch01 event with the time-line formula (not a second reveal). Correct the two answer keys to "the note had been Jake's cup label and stuck to the lid".

**B-10 · Major · Ch12 (and reused Ch16, Ch17, Ch20, Appendix I-12) · essential for the taught chunk**
- **Original:** The whiteboard line "My point is…, for one reason:… Take…". Also "For one reason: in cold weather people ask for something warm before anything else."
- **Problem:** *For one reason:* is not idiomatic as a freestanding opener. Natural options are *for one simple reason*, *for one thing*, or *My main reason is that…*. A native reader will hear it as slightly stilted. It is also the central chunk of the chapter and is reused later.
- **Fix:** Teach "My point is that… The main reason is simple: … Take…" or "For one simple reason: …". Update the whiteboard line, the answer key, the skeleton and I-12.
- **Related, Optional (see C-11):** *My point is…* often signals impatience (*my point is, we're late*). *I'd argue that…* or *My main point is…* reads more neutral as a PREP opener.

**B-11 · Major · Ch17 §17.2, §17.4, 翻车彩蛋区, key ①**
- **Problem:** The chapter teaches "I'll make three points: First… Second… Third… Then I'll take questions." The 翻车 block criticises "I'll make three points: First… Second… Moving on… The tricky bit…" because "听完不知道第三点去哪了". But the chapter's own demonstration (§17.4) is exactly that pattern: "First… Moving on to the second point… The tricky bit is… To wrap up". The answer key lists the five sections as "开场 / First / Moving on / The tricky bit / To wrap up", which differs again from §17.2's "First / Second / Third (and the tricky bit) / To wrap up".
- **Fix:** Make the demonstration say "First… Second… Third, and this is the tricky bit… To wrap up…" and make the key match §17.2.

**B-12 · Major · Ch18 §18.1, §18.2, §18.4, comic table · essential**
- **Original:** "Same message, three outfits." The three samples are:
  - "Kettle's back. — Zhou"
  - "Hi Zoe, could you check Sunday's slot for me? Thanks!"
  - "Dear Ms Reed, I was wondering whether a twin room would be available on the 12th…"
- **Problem:** These are three different messages, not one message in three registers. The chapter's premise ("距离在读者，不在你") is therefore not demonstrated. Zhou's and Zoe's comment that "it's more distance, not more polite" has nothing to compare against.
- **Fix:** Pick one message and write it three ways. For example: asking for a room or lending a charger → sticky note / message / email. The §18.6 task already does this with the charger, which is the right model.
- **Also in this chapter** (see C-9):
  - the "12th" vs "by Thursday" deadline
  - word counts
  - "好读三倍"
  - the second 翻车 contradicting §18.5

**B-13 · Major · Timeline wording across chapters · essential**
- **Original examples:**
  - 7.4 "Last year that reflex had cost him a forty-minute walk around a building with a robot."
  - 8.4 "A year ago he would have produced a long, foggy sentence…"
  - 12.4 "A year ago he would have written three pages of notes…"
  - 16.4 "A year ago Zhou would have heard 'you're wrong'…"
  - 8.4 "had been in Zhou's bag for three chapters" (I-08 says "three weeks")
  - 20.4 "a habit nineteen chapters old"
- **Problem:** The plot clearly runs weeks, not years (Ch01 is "First week in the office"; the book later says "week one … week seven"; Ch15 "a quarter ago"). "A year ago" and "last year" contradict this. "Chapters" inside a narrative also breaks the fourth wall unevenly (sometimes weeks, sometimes chapters). In 7.4 the "forty-minute walk" did not happen; in Ch03 Zhou *avoided* it.
- **Fix:** Use one clock. Suggested: chapter = week. Replace "A year ago/Last year" with "A few weeks ago / In week one", and replace "chapters" with "weeks" inside the fiction (or mark them as deliberate meta jokes).

**B-14 · Major · Character name (Ch18, I-18, bible)**
- **Original:** "Kind regards, Zhou Lin" (18.1, I-18). Elsewhere: 周 Yizhou, "I'm Yizhou. I go by Zhou." The character bible lists the 13-year-old as "Maya Lin".
- **Problem:** If 周 is the surname, "Zhou Lin" is wrong. If "Lin" is a surname, it collides with Maya's. A formal email signature is exactly where the ambiguity matters.
- **Fix:** Decide the full name. Suggested "Zhou Yizhou" (not great) or "Yizhou Zhou", and use it consistently. Check I-01 ("I go by Lin") as well.

**B-15 · Major · Model essays and task wording (Ch19, Ch20, C-1, C-2, A-1)**
- **Problem:** The model essays are much longer than their stated lengths.
  - C-1 is titled "400 词议论文完整范文". My manual count is about 560 words.
  - C-2 says "本篇约 550 词", against a task of 500–600 words. My manual count is about 1,000 words (both halves together).
- **Also:** Tasks and headings use 字 for English length ("400 字议论文", "500–600 字结业文", "250 字比较文"). English length should be 词.
- **Why it matters:** Learners will measure themselves against a model that is up to twice the required length.
- **Fix:** Run a real count. Trim C-1 to about 400 and C-2 to 500–600, or change the stated task length. Replace 字 with 词 throughout the English tasks.

**B-17 · Major · Appendix H (lemma index) and the "515" figure**
- **Problem:** The appendix says entries are dictionary base forms, "不做机械词干截断". But the index contains inflected duplicates and non-lemmas:

  - Inflected forms such as `hits`, `rains`, `depends`, `worries`, `ears`, `ones`, `boxes`, `shows`.

  - Day names in lowercase (`monday` … `friday`) as families.

- **Why it matters:** The "515 个词族 (A 238 / R 277), 105 复现" headline is quoted in the companion as a deliverable. As built, it cannot be trusted.
- **Fix:** Re-run with a real lemmatiser, remove proper nouns and A1/A2 stock words, then restate the totals (or drop the exact figure and say "about").

## F. What Should Be Preserved

- **The dialogue voice.** Short, witty, and recognisably spoken. Jake's dry understatement, Zoe's blunt coaching lines and Maya's deadpan interruptions work, and learners can actually reuse them.
- **The "buy time" chunks and the repair ladder** (*Sorry — give me a sec → Did you mean A or B? → So I go…, did I get that right?*). This is the book's best teaching idea and it is consistently reinforced across chapters.
- **The one-line whiteboard ("Today's Line") device.** Memorable, reusable, and strong for spaced recall.
- **Humour and cultural jokes** (the thermos, "I'm fine, thank you, and you?" as a "polite stone wall", "hijacking my dinner"). They are not decoration; they carry the register lessons.
- **Honest limitation language.** The disclaimers on fictional data (Ch14), on the hot-drink claim (Ch01) and on B2 as a target are good practice. Keep them, and bring the front-matter wording (B-19) in line.
- **Recycling across chapters.** The staircase (Ch09 → Ch12 → Ch16), the dotted line (Ch14 → Ch15) and the empty card (Ch17 → Ch20) give the 20 chapters a genuine learning arc.
- **Grammar explanations that are accurate and simple.** The `I'd rather`, hardly ever / adverb position, third conditional and `I was wondering whether` explanations are correct and need no change.
- **The "翻车彩蛋区" format.** The errors it shows are real learner errors, and the corrections given are right (with the Ch17 and Ch18 exceptions noted above).
